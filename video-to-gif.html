GIF settings

GIFs are limited to 20 seconds. Smaller width and fewer frames make much smaller files.

Making GIFs that look good and stay small

GIF is an old format: it stores every frame with at most 256 colours and compresses poorly, so a few seconds of full-HD video can easily become 30 MB. The tricks are to keep clips short (2–6 seconds), use 12–15 frames per second and a width of 480 px or less. This maker builds an optimised colour palette from your clip and refreshes it as the scene changes, which keeps colours accurate.

Where GIFs still win

  • Slack, Teams, Discord and email, where a GIF plays inline everywhere without a play button.
  • GitHub READMEs and documentation, to show a feature in action.
  • Reaction clips and memes.

For websites, a short muted MP4 or WebM is 5–10× smaller than the same GIF; the video compressor can make one.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a watermark?

No. GIFs are made on your device with no watermark.

Why is my GIF so large?

GIF compression is inefficient. Shorten the clip, lower the frame rate to 8–12 fps and reduce the width to 320–480 px.

Can I make a GIF from a YouTube video?

Only from video files on your device. Downloading YouTube videos is against YouTube's terms.
