    Settings

    Which image format should I use?

    JPG is the universal choice for photos and works everywhere. PNG is lossless and keeps transparency, so use it for logos, screenshots and graphics with text. WebP makes photos 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same quality and is supported by every modern browser, which makes it ideal for websites. AVIF compresses even better than WebP but takes longer to encode and isn't accepted everywhere yet.

    How this tool works

    Your images are opened directly by your browser, drawn onto a canvas at the size you choose and re-encoded in the new format. Large reductions are done in several half-size steps, which keeps detail sharper than a single resize. Phone photos are rotated correctly using their EXIF orientation. Everything happens on your computer or phone, so it is fast even for big batches and your photos stay private.

    Bonus: location data is removed

    Photos from phones often contain hidden metadata such as GPS coordinates, the date and the camera model. Re-encoding removes it, so converted or compressed images are safer to share publicly.

    Tips for the best results

    • For websites, resize to the largest size the image is actually displayed at (often 1600–2400 px wide) and export WebP at 75–85% quality.
    • For email attachments, a longest side of 1600 px at 75% JPG quality keeps most photos under 400 KB.
    • Keep screenshots and graphics as PNG: JPG adds blurry artefacts around text.
    • HEIC photos from iPhones can be opened in Safari. On other browsers, set your iPhone to Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are my images uploaded to a server?

    No. Conversion, compression and resizing run entirely in your browser. Your photos never leave your device, which is also why it works offline once the page has loaded.

    Is there a limit on the number or size of images?

    There is no fixed limit. Batches of hundreds of photos work fine on a laptop; very large images (above about 50 megapixels) may be slow on older phones.

    Will converting reduce the quality?

    JPG, WebP and AVIF are lossy formats, so each re-encode discards some detail. At 80–90% quality the difference is invisible for most photos. PNG is lossless.

    Can I convert HEIC to JPG?

    Yes, in every modern browser. Safari decodes HEIC itself; in Chrome, Edge and Firefox a HEIC decoder (libheif) loads automatically the first time you add an iPhone photo. Our dedicated HEIC to JPG converter is set up for exactly this.

    Does it keep transparency?

    PNG, WebP and AVIF keep transparent areas. JPG has no transparency, so transparent areas are filled with the background colour you choose.
