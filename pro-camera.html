Common Movie & Video Aspect Ratios

In cinematographic storytelling, your aspect ratio dictates composition, emotional intimacy, and spatial scale. 1.85:1 (Flat) Theatrical

The standard theatrical widescreen format used in U.S. cinemas for spherical lens productions. Provides a natural balance of vertical headroom and horizontal breadth, favored for dialogue-driven dramas, comedies, and character studies.

2.39:1 / 2.40:1 (Scope) Anamorphic

The ultra-wide anamorphic format commonly used for epics, action films, and sweeping landscapes. Creates dramatic horizontal depth, signature oval bokeh, and cinematic letterbox framing.

16:9 (1.78:1) Universal

The universal standard for modern high-definition televisions, computer monitors, and online streaming platforms. Offers full-sensor efficiency with zero letterboxing on standard displays.

4:3 (1.33:1) Vintage Box

The classic vintage box format used in early silent cinema, academy ratio films, and vintage CRT televisions. Frequently deployed by auteur filmmakers for nostalgic intimacy and vertical portrait power.

IMAX (1.43:1 & 1.90:1) Immersive

A much taller format that provides a grand, vertically immersive field of view in specialized theaters. 1.43:1 reproduces 70mm 15-perf film frames, while 1.90:1 represents modern digital IMAX auditoriums.

9:16 (Vertical) Mobile Cine

Full-height vertical cinematography format engineered for mobile screens, Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts.
