What this webcam test measures

Most online camera tests only show your picture and the resolution the browser asked for. This one looks at the frames your camera actually delivers and grades each part of the image the way a video engineer would. It takes a downscaled copy of each frame (320 pixels wide), runs the numbers in JavaScript, and throws the frame away. No image ever leaves your device.

📐 Resolution

This is the size of the video stream your camera is sending right now. Many “1080p” webcams fall back to 720p or lower when the USB bus is busy, when another app holds the camera, or when the light is poor. 1920×1080 scores full marks and 1280×720 is fine for most video calls. Anything lower will look soft in a full-screen meeting.

⏱️ Frame rate

Frame rate is counted from the frames actually delivered to the page, not taken from the label on the box. Webcams deliberately slow down in dim rooms so they can expose each frame for longer. If you see 15 fps here, adding light in front of you is usually the quickest way back to a smooth 30 fps.

💡 Exposure and clipping

Exposure is the average brightness of the frame. A well-lit face usually lands between about 37% and 69%. Clipping is the share of pixels that are pure white (blown highlights) or pure black (crushed shadows), where all detail is lost. A bright window behind you is the classic cause: the camera exposes for the window and your face goes dark. Switch on Zebra to see blown-out areas striped in pink.

🔍 Sharpness

Sharpness is estimated with the variance of a Laplacian filter, a standard focus measure that rises with fine edge detail. The value depends on what is in the frame, so compare readings in the same spot: wipe the lens, re-run the check and see if the number goes up. Smudged lenses, sitting too close for autofocus, and heavy noise reduction in low light are the usual culprits.

🎨 Colour balance

The test compares the average red, green and blue levels to spot a warm (orange), cool (blue), green or magenta cast. Mixing daylight from a window with warm indoor bulbs is the most common cause, because the camera cannot white-balance for both at once. Using a single type of light, ideally a daylight-balanced 5000–5600 K bulb, fixes most casts.

How the score works

Each measurement gets a score from 0 to 100. The overall score is a weighted average in which exposure, sharpness and resolution count the most, because those are what other people notice first on a call. 75 or more means meeting ready, 90 or more means studio ready. The live score updates as you move; the 5-second check averages around 35 measurements for a steadier result you can copy or download.

Quick fixes that raise your score

  1. Face the light. Put a window or lamp in front of you, behind the screen, never behind you. See our lighting and setup guide.
  2. Clean the lens. A fingerprint is enough to make the whole picture look hazy.
  3. Close other camera apps. Zoom, Teams, OBS and browser tabs can hold the camera and force lower resolutions. The troubleshooting guide covers this in detail.
  4. Use a USB 3 port and avoid unpowered hubs for 1080p and 4K webcams.
  5. Upgrade for free: a modern phone camera beats almost every laptop webcam. Here is how to use your phone as a webcam.
  6. Still blurry? Work through how to fix a blurry webcam.

Webcam test FAQ

Is this webcam test private?

Yes. The analysis runs entirely in your browser using the standard camera API and an off-screen canvas. Frames are never recorded, stored or uploaded, and the camera turns off as soon as you press Stop or close the tab.

Why does my 1080p webcam show 720p?

The camera may be connected to a slow USB port or hub, another app may already be using it, or the browser may have picked a lower mode to keep the frame rate up. Close other camera apps, try a different port and run the test again.

Why is my frame rate low?

In dim light most webcams lengthen the exposure of each frame, which caps the frame rate at around 15 fps. Add light in front of you. A busy CPU and power-saving modes on laptops can also reduce it.

Does the score work on phones?

Yes. It works in current versions of Chrome, Safari, Edge, Firefox and Samsung Internet. If your device has several cameras, pick one from the camera menu after starting the test.

Can I test my microphone too?

Yes. Use our online mic test for levels, noise and a 5-second playback check. Then try your camera with live filters in the WebcamClicks studio.
