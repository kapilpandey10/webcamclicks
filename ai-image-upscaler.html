How AI upscaling differs from resizing

Ordinary resizing (in Paint, Photoshop's bicubic or any website resizer) estimates new pixels by averaging their neighbours, so an enlarged photo looks soft and blocky. An AI super-resolution model has learned from millions of image pairs what sharp detail looks like, and redraws edges, hair, text and textures at the new size. WebcamClicks uses Swin2SR, a transformer-based model from the research community, converted to run in your browser.

Which model to choose

  • Photo, real-world (4×) is trained on real camera noise and blur: best for old phone photos, webcam snapshots and pictures saved from chats.
  • Compressed JPEG (4×) also removes the blocky 8×8 artifacts of heavily compressed images, such as screenshots of screenshots or images from social media.
  • Graphics, screenshots, text (2×) keeps lines and lettering crisp for logos, UI screenshots and diagrams.

Need 2× from a photo? Choose the 4× photo model with output size 2×: the result is usually sharper than a 2× model.

Speed and limits

The model (about 20–60 MB) downloads in the background when you open the page and is then cached. With a graphics chip (WebGPU), a typical photo takes seconds; on the processor alone, larger photos can take a minute or two. To keep memory in check, very large photos are processed at up to about 1.2 megapixels for 4× (giving roughly 19 megapixels out). For quick fixes of exposure and colour without enlarging, use the photo enhancer.

Like every WebcamClicks tool, this runs entirely in your browser. Your files are never uploaded, so it is fast, private and free from upload limits.

Frequently asked questions

Is the AI upscaler free?

Yes, with no credits, watermark or sign-up.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. The AI model runs in your browser; only the model itself is downloaded once.

Can it make a blurry photo sharp?

It restores detail when a photo is small or slightly soft. It cannot recover a photo that is badly out of focus or shaken.

Does it invent details?

Like every AI upscaler it fills in plausible fine detail, so very small faces or text may not be exactly what the camera saw. Use it for display and print, not as evidence.
