🔥 Trending Filters (8)

These looks are built on a colour-grading engine that uses tone-curve lookup tables, split toning and film grain that stays stable from frame to frame, instead of simple colour overlays. Every sample below is the actual filter output on the same unedited portrait.

📷 Y2K Digicam

Early-2000s point-and-shoot cameras used tiny CCD sensors. Their photos clip highlights early, keep shadows slightly cool and add crunchy in-camera sharpening. Y2K Digicam recreates that look by processing your video at a lower "sensor" resolution, grading it with a cool-shadow tone curve, adding colour noise in the darks and stamping the date in orange, as those cameras did.

Best for: Selfies, outfit photos, friend groups and nostalgic "photo dump" posts.

Try Y2K Digicam live ➔

⚡ Direct Flash

Pointing a bare flash straight at your subject gives that glossy, editorial party look: a bright face, a background that drops into darkness and specular highlights on skin. The filter models flash falloff (light weakens with distance from the subject), rolls off highlights gently instead of clipping them and adds a subtle sheen.

Best for: Night-out vibes, fashion-style portraits and dark rooms where you want to pop out of the frame.

Try Direct Flash live ➔

🌃 Night Tungsten 800

Tungsten-balanced cinema film is famous at night for its cool shadows and the red glow ("halation") that forms around bright lights. Night Tungsten 800 uses a teal split-tone grade, real luminance-weighted grain and two layers of red halation: a tight core and a wide spill.

Best for: Shooting under lamps, neon or fairy lights, and moody evening video calls.

Try Night Tungsten 800 live ➔

🌫️ Dreamcore Glow

Dreamcore is the soft, slightly unreal aesthetic of half-remembered places. The grade lifts the blacks into lavender, pushes highlights toward peach, lowers saturation and adds a strong bloom with a hazy white edge.

Best for: Soft portraits, aesthetic edits and dreamy short clips.

Try Dreamcore Glow live ➔

🖨️ Risograph Print

Risograph printers lay down one ink at a time, so zines and posters printed on them show visible halftone dots, bold fluorescent colours and slight mis-registration. The filter separates your image into fluorescent pink and medium blue inks, prints each as an angled halftone screen and offsets them slightly on warm uncoated paper.

Best for: Poster-style portraits, profile pictures and graphic-design moodboards.

Try Risograph Print live ➔

🟪 Pixel Dither

Ordered (Bayer) dithering is how early computers and games faked smooth gradients with only a few colours. Pixel Dither reduces your video to four tones (midnight, violet, rose and cream) and uses an 8×8 threshold matrix to build shading from tiny pixel patterns.

Best for: Album-cover style shots, retro-game avatars and stylised stream overlays.

Try Pixel Dither live ➔

🌈 Pixel Sort

Pixel sorting is a classic glitch-art technique. In each column, the filter finds runs of pixels inside a brightness band and sorts them so bright colours melt downward in streaks. The threshold slowly breathes, so the streaks grow and recede as you watch.

Best for: Music-video clips, experimental art and eye-catching transitions.

Try Pixel Sort live ➔

🎨 Toon Shade

Cel shading is how animation turns smooth lighting into flat bands of colour with ink outlines. Toon Shade softens skin, quantises brightness into five smooth-edged shading steps and draws outlines from a Sobel edge detector.

Best for: Cartoon avatars, fun video calls and comic-style photo strips.

Try Toon Shade live ➔

How to get the best result from any filter

  • Light your face from the front. A window or lamp in front of you (not behind) gives every filter cleaner detail to work with. Our lighting and setup guide covers this in depth.
  • Use the strength slider. Most looks are at their most natural between 60% and 85% strength, especially film and portrait grades.
  • Match the filter to the moment. Film and portrait looks suit photo strips. Animated sci-fi, cosmic and glitch effects shine in short video clips.
  • Blurry image? Filters can't add detail the camera never captured. See how to fix a blurry webcam first.

Frequently asked questions

Are these webcam filters free?

Yes. Every filter on WebcamClicks is free to use with no sign-up, no watermark and no download.

Is my camera video uploaded anywhere?

No. All filters run locally in your browser using Canvas and WebGL. Your video never leaves your device, and photos are only saved when you choose to download them.

Which filters are trending right now?

The Trending category collects the looks people are currently sharing most: Y2K Digicam, Direct Flash, Night Tungsten 800, Dreamcore Glow, Risograph Print, Pixel Dither, Pixel Sort and Toon Shade.

Do the filters work on phones?

Yes. The filters are tuned to run in real time on modern iPhones and Android phones. A few of the heaviest effects are only shown on desktop computers.

Can I use these filters in Zoom, Google Meet or Teams?

WebcamClicks runs in your browser tab, so you can record clips or take photos with any filter. To use a filter as your live meeting camera you would need a virtual-camera tool; the filters themselves do not install anything on your computer.
